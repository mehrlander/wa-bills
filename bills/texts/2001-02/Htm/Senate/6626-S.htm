Requiring the Washington state institute for public policy to review and evaluate whether branch campuses are fulfilling their intended role.


    AN ACT Relating to a study of the role, mission, and structure of branch campuses by the Washington state institute for public policy; and creating a new section.

 

BE IT ENACTED BY THE LEGISLATURE OF THE STATE OF WASHINGTON:

 

    NEW SECTION.  Sec. 1.  In 1989, the legislature created a system of branch campuses to meet the upper division and graduate education needs of individuals, businesses, and urban communities.  Over a decade later it is time to review the system and to determine whether or not the original intent is being met as well as to determine whether the original goals are appropriate in the economic and educational climate of today.

    The lack of sufficient educational opportunities in urban areas caused the 1989 legislature to endorse the creation of branch campuses for the University of Washington and Washington State University.  The new campuses were expected to meet the upper-division and graduate needs of place-bound individuals who were unable to relocate due to financial difficulties, family commitments, or job responsibilities.  The branch campuses were to be operated as models of a two-plus-two educational system.  In addition, the new campuses were expected to provide the educational opportunities that are beneficial to the economy of the state's urban centers not previously served by a four-year institution.  The new campuses were also expected to address the issue of baccalaureate degree production in Washington falling behind other states in the nation.

    The Washington state institute for public policy shall review and evaluate the following questions that surround the branch campuses of the University of Washington and Washington State University:  (1) What evidence is there to support that the branch campuses fulfilled the original intent of the legislature; (2) to what extent do key factors such as student demographics; demand for and availability of upper division and graduate higher education; and local and state labor markets indicate a need to modify the role, mission, or structure of the branch campuses; (3) what has been the experience of other states with upper division branch campuses; (4) what is the relationship of the branch campuses to their local community colleges; and (5) what are some policy options the legislature could consider regarding the future of branch campuses?  In completing the report, the institute may request information from appropriate outside resources such as the western interstate commission for higher education, the national conference of state legislatures, the education commission of the states, or the national center for higher education management systems.

    The institute shall submit a report to the appropriate legislative committees by December 12, 2002.
