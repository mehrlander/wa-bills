     AN ACT Relating to prohibiting the administration of credit card applications on campuses of institutions of higher education; adding a new section to chapter 28B.10 RCW; creating a new section; and providing an effective date.

BE IT ENACTED BY THE LEGISLATURE OF THE STATE OF WASHINGTON:

NEW SECTION.  Sec. 1   The legislature finds that eighty-one percent of students have received a credit card by the end of the first year of college, and that the high interest rates on student credit cards, coupled with their ease of use and a poor understanding of how credit works, often leads to runaway and unmanageable debt. The legislature also finds that students routinely apply for credit cards simply to get free merchandise, but two or three years later, students may begin to receive collection notices for credit cards they requested but never received or used. Students in North Carolina who seek assistance from student legal services about debt problems own an average of four and seventy-seven one-hundredths credit cards with an average balance of seven thousand dollars each. This amount of credit card debt is in addition to any loans the student is obligated to pay back after graduation. The legislature further finds that many states have found success in regulating credit card companies' ability to solicit business from college students by controlling the conditions of soliciting and mandating certain behavior from the institutions of higher education. Lawmakers in ten states have introduced legislation to try and rein in credit card companies' solicitation of college students and to increase students' access to educational programs about credit and financial management. The purposes of this act are to encourage young adults to obtain credit cards in a more responsible manner and to keep young adults out of credit card debt. The legislature finds that due to the first amendment rights in the United States Constitution, this act cannot prohibit the soliciting of the credit card companies, but will ban the administering of credit card applications on college campuses.

NEW SECTION.  Sec. 2   A new section is added to chapter 28B.10 RCW to read as follows:
     (1) Institutions of higher education shall ban the administration of credit card applications on site of a college campus and shall encourage young adults to establish credit in a more responsible manner.     
     (2) The definitions in this section apply throughout this section unless the context clearly requires otherwise.
     (a) "Ban" means to prohibit, not allowing without any exception, not having any right to do so, and banishing the right of.
     (b) "On site of a college campus" means on any and all property owned by the institution of higher education itself, within any borders of the belonging property of the institution.
     (c) "Administering" means the act of handing out or giving to, or allowing one to get a hold of an application.
     (d) "Application" means any form or piece of information that allows one to apply or sign up for a credit card, and any way and/or means that commits a student to the credit card requirements or its commitments.

NEW SECTION.  Sec. 3   If any provision of this act or its application to any person or circumstance is held invalid, the remainder of the act or the application of the provision to other persons or circumstances is not affected.

NEW SECTION.  Sec. 4   This act takes effect January 1, 2004.
