Preventing bomb making by minors.

    AN ACT Relating to preventing access by minors to materials about bomb making; adding a new chapter to Title 9 RCW; prescribing penalties; and declaring an emergency.

 

BE IT ENACTED BY THE LEGISLATURE OF THE STATE OF WASHINGTON:

 

    NEW SECTION.  Sec. 1.  The legislature finds that an ever-increasing amount of information on making explosive devices such as bombs is being made readily available to an ever-growing number of people, including young children.  Minors are being given access to information on how to make explosive devices with such specificity and sufficiency that they are able to quickly assemble a bomb with otherwise legal and easily obtainable materials and cause it to be detonated.

    There is no right, constitutional or otherwise, to make or possess bombs and government should exercise its legitimate authority to ensure the safety of its citizens.  The legislature intends to exercise its police powers to maintain the safety of children and protect the public at large by ensuring access to information regarding explosive devices and the use or disposal of such devices by minors are properly authorized and supervised.

 

    NEW SECTION.  Sec. 2.  The definitions in this section apply throughout this chapter unless the context clearly requires otherwise.

    (1) "Bomb" means any device containing an explosive substance and capable of being exploded.  "Bomb" does not include fireworks or small arms ammunition or small arms primers as defined and authorized under state or federal law.

    (2) "Instructions" does not include information clearly and obviously intended for safety instructional purposes only.

    (3) "Knowledge" means knowledge as defined in RCW 9A.08.010.

    (4) "Knowledge of its character" means that the person has knowledge that the material contains, depicts, or describes instructions about explosive devices or substances with such specificity and sufficiency that a person using the information would be able to assemble a bomb and cause it to be detonated.  Such knowledge may be proved by direct or circumstantial evidence, or both.

    (5) "Material" means a picture film or a publication, or any combination thereof.

    (6) "Minor" means any person under the age of eighteen years.

    (7) "Person" means any individual, partnership, firm, association, corporation, or other legal entity.

    (8) "Picture film" means any film, film or plate negative, film or plate positive, film designed to be projected on a screen for exhibition, glass slides or transparencies, either in negative or positive form, designed for exhibition by projection on a screen, videotape, or any other medium used to electronically transmit or reproduce images on a screen.

    (9) "Publication" means any book, magazine, article, pamphlet, writing, printing illustration, picture, sound recording, electronic transmission, or telephonic communication.

 

    NEW SECTION.  Sec. 3.  No person may, with knowledge of its character, provide materials about bomb making in such a way that minors, as part of the invited general public, are able to access the material.  However, a person is deemed not to have provided access to materials about bomb making to minors if:

    (1) The materials about bomb making are kept in an area that is not accessible to minors without the consent of the minor's parent or guardian;

    (2) In the case of an operator who transmits materials about bomb making via cable television transmissions, the operator first notifies the subscriber of the availability of a device that allows a subscriber to prohibit access to the viewing of a particular cable transmission;

    (3) In the case of a person who provides a minor with access to a computer containing material about bomb making that is stored locally, or that can be connected to the internet, the computer is equipped with specialized filtering software actively installed for the purpose of protecting minors from accessing materials about bomb making;

    (4) In the case of a person publishing a world wide web page containing materials about bomb making, the web page contains codes or hidden comments that trigger the blocking mechanisms of any browser-filtering software that is designed for the purpose of protecting minors from accessing materials about bomb making;

    (5) In the case of a person hosting or mirroring internet content on servers located within the state of Washington, the person mandates the inclusion in all web pages of codes or hidden comments that trigger the blocking mechanisms of any browser-filtering software that is designed for the purpose of protecting minors from accessing materials about bomb making; or

    (6) In the case of a person providing electronic communications or telecommunications access or connection to or from a facility, system, or network, whether one-way or interactive, including transmission, downloading, storage, navigational tools, and related capabilities that are incidental to the provision of the electronic communications or telecommunications access or connection, the person mandates the inclusion in all web pages of codes or hidden comments that trigger the blocking mechanisms of any browser-filtering software that is designed for the purpose of protecting minors from accessing materials about bomb making, or to sell, furnish, present, distribute, allow to view or hear, or otherwise disseminate to a minor, with or without consideration, any materials about bomb making.

 

    NEW SECTION.  Sec. 4.  In any prosecution for violation of section 3 of this act, it is an affirmative defense that:

    (1) The materials about bomb making were displayed or otherwise disseminated to a minor by the minor's parent or legal guardian, for a bona fide purpose;

    (2) The person made a reasonable good faith attempt to ascertain the true age of the minor by requiring production of a driver's license, marriage license, birth certificate, or other governmental or educational identification card or paper, or copy thereof if supplied by mail or electronic facsimile when in-person production thereof is impractical, and not relying solely on the oral allegations or apparent age of the minor; or

    (3) If engaged in the commercial distribution of materials about bomb making by electronic or telephonic transmission, access by persons under eighteen years of age is restricted by requiring use of a verified credit card, debit account, adult access code, or adult personal identification number before transmission of the material.

 

    NEW SECTION.  Sec. 5.  (1) A person who is convicted of violating section 3 of this act is guilty of a gross misdemeanor punishable under RCW 9.92.020.

    (2) Each day that a violation of section 3 of this act occurs or continues is a separate offense and is punishable as a separate violation.

    (3) Every act, thing, or transaction prohibited by section 3 of this act is a separate offense as to each item involved and is punishable as such.

    (4) For the purpose of this section, multiple copies of the same identical item, or other such identical materials are a single offense.

 

    NEW SECTION.  Sec. 6.  (1) Any person who is injured by a bomb,  any person who is a parent, guardian, or sibling of a person who has been injured or killed by a bomb, and the estate of a person who has been killed by a bomb has a cause of action against:

    (a) Any person who committed a violation of section 3 of this act and as a result of the violation the person was injured or killed; and

    (b) Any parent or guardian of a minor who knew that a minor under his or her control possessed a bomb without the parent's or guardian's consent or not for a bona fide purpose, or who reasonably should have known that a minor under his or her control possessed a bomb for other than a bona fide purpose, and who failed to ensure the bomb was disposed of in a lawful manner, and as a result of the failure the person was injured or killed.

    (2) In such an action, in addition to damages for medical, hospital, medication expenses, and loss of services and support, damages may be recovered for pain and suffering and the loss of love and companionship of the person in such amount as, under all the circumstances of the case, may be just.

 

    NEW SECTION.  Sec. 7.  If any provision of this act or its application to any person or circumstance is held invalid, the remainder of the act or the application of the provision to other persons or circumstances is not affected.

 

    NEW SECTION.  Sec. 8.  This act is necessary for the immediate preservation of the public peace, health, or safety, or support of the state government and its existing public institutions, and takes effect immediately.

 

    NEW SECTION.  Sec. 9.  Sections 1 through 8 of this act constitute a new chapter in Title 9 RCW.
