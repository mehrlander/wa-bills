Providing licensing standards for interpreters for the deaf.

    AN ACT Relating to the licensure of interpreters for the deaf and hard of hearing; adding a new chapter to Title 72 RCW; prescribing penalties; and providing an effective date.

 

BE IT ENACTED BY THE LEGISLATURE OF THE STATE OF WASHINGTON:

 

    NEW SECTION.  Sec. 1.  The definitions in this section apply throughout this chapter unless the context clearly requires otherwise.

    (1) "Committee" means the Washington interpreters for the deaf and hard of hearing policy committee.

    (2) "Consumer" means a person who is deaf or hard of hearing or who requires special communication techniques in order to communicate.

    (3) "Interpreter" means a person who engages in the act of or practice of interpreting for gain in the state of Washington, but does not include a relay interpreter unless by rule adopted by the secretary of the department of social and health services, at the request of the committee, a system of certifying relay interpreters is instituted.

    (4) "Interpreting" means the act of translating or transliterating English concepts to any or all necessary specialized language used by a consumer or the act of translating a consumer's specialized vocabulary to English concepts.

    (5) "Nationally recognized certification" means certification by the registry of interpreters for the deaf or the national association of the deaf.

    (6) "Relay interpreter" means an intermediary interpreter with the necessary specialized vocabulary to relay sign language concepts from an interpreter to deaf consumers who have minimal English language and sign language skills.

    (7) "Specialized vocabulary" means manual signs and gestures operating as visual or tactile ways of communicating thoughts, ideas, and feelings, including but not limited to, American sign language, English-based sign language, cued speech, oral interpreting, manually coded English, and contact sign language.

 

    NEW SECTION.  Sec. 2.  (1) Effective December 1, 2001, no person may represent himself or herself as an interpreter or engage in the practice of interpreting unless certified in accordance with this chapter, and no person or public or private entity may knowingly employ an uncertified interpreter to engage in the act of interpreting.  A certified interpreter must have adequate professional liability insurance coverage, have passed a criminal background check, and be registered with the office of deaf and hard of hearing services, or its successor authority.  The office of deaf and hard of hearing services, or its successor authority, shall determine the adequacy of professional liability insurance coverage and shall certify any person as an interpreter who submits evidence of nationally recognized certification, proof of adequate professional liability insurance coverage, and payment of a reasonable fee, which may be used only for the necessary costs of administering this chapter.

    (2) This chapter does not apply to:

    (a) Interpreters working at religious activities;

    (b) Interpreters working as volunteers without compensation, except for volunteers interpreting for state agency offices and programs;

    (c) Interpreters working in an emergency.  For purposes of this section, "emergency" means a situation in which an adult who receives the interpretation services decides that the delay necessary to obtain a certified interpreter is likely to cause him or her injury or loss to the consumer and signs a waiver holding all persons harmless.  The waiver shall clearly state that the adult who receives the interpretation services knows he or she has a right to interpretation by a certified interpreter and willingly waives this right;

    (d) Family members who do not interpret for compensation;

    (e) Interpreters employed in public schools, covered under section 7 of this act; or

    (f) The activities and services of an interpreter intern or a student in training who is:

    (i) Enrolled in a program of study in interpreting at a recognized program of interpreter training or who has graduated from a recognized program of interpreter training within the previous two years;

    (ii) Interpreting under the supervision of a certified interpreter as part of a supervised program of study; or

    (iii) Identified as an interpreter intern or student in training.

    (3) A person who resides out of Washington state, who has a nationally recognized certification, may interpret for up to twenty days without being certified as an interpreter under this chapter.

 

    NEW SECTION.  Sec. 3.  (1) The Washington interpreters for the deaf and hard of hearing policy committee is created, consisting of ten members as follows:

    (a) The president of the Washington state association of the deaf or the president's designee;

    (b) The director of the office of deaf and hard of hearing services or the director's designee;

    (c) A representative from the office of the superintendent of public instruction, appointed by the superintendent of public instruction;

    (d) A consumer of interpreting services appointed by the secretary of the department of social and health services; and

    (e) One representative appointed by each of the following groups:

    (i) The Washington state association of the deaf, interpreters chapter;

    (ii) The Washington state registry of interpreters for the deaf;

    (iii) The American sign language teachers association;

    (iv) The Washington school for the deaf;

    (v) The Washington deaf and blind citizens; and

    (vi) Self-help for the hard of hearing.

    (2) If one of these groups fails to appoint a person, the committee shall select a person from that group to represent the group.  Terms shall be established by rule adopted by the secretary of the department of social and health services.

    (3) The members of the committee shall not receive compensation for their services on the committee.  All members, other than the director or designee of the office of deaf and hard of hearing services, shall be reimbursed for actual and necessary expenses incurred in the performance of their duties.

 

    NEW SECTION.  Sec. 4.  The committee shall:

    (1) Set a reasonable fee to be charged for the issuance of interpreter certification;

    (2) Provide a biennial report to the legislature on the effectiveness of this chapter; and

    (3) Provide to the office of deaf and hard of hearing services, or its successor authority, beginning on October 1st of each year, an ongoing review of professional development and support systems for interpreters, including a review of:

    (a) Existing public and private education programs and training resources within the state;

    (b) The current number of certified interpreters; and

    (c) Areas of interpreter shortage based upon geographic areas and types of interpreting, such as mental health, educational, and ethnic diversity.

 

    NEW SECTION.  Sec. 5.  The office of deaf and hard of hearing services, or its successor authority, and each regional deaf service center shall retain in their files a copy of the code of professional ethics and the grievance procedures for both the national association of the deaf and the registry of interpreters for the deaf.

 

    NEW SECTION.  Sec. 6.  An interpreter who has had her or his certification suspended or revoked as a result of a grievance action through either the national association of the deaf or the registry of interpreters for the deaf may not work as an interpreter until that certification has been reinstated.

 

    NEW SECTION.  Sec. 7.  Educational interpreters who are employed in the public school system must successfully complete a thirty-hour training course offered through the office of the superintendent of public instruction.  Commencing in the year 2002, any person newly employed by the public school system as an educational interpreter must be certified as an interpreter and have completed the educational training offered by the office of the superintendent of public instruction.  If an educational interpreter for a public school system has been employed in that capacity for four or more years, with satisfactory evaluations and without a period of more than six months between employment in the public school system, the employee may be retained by the school district as an educational interpreter without meeting these standards.  If a school district is unable to find a certified interpreter, the school district may request a nonrenewable one-year waiver from the office of the superintendent of public instruction and must demonstrate that efforts have been made to seek an appropriately qualified person.  The office of deaf and hard of hearing services, or its successor authority, shall annually provide to the superintendent of public instruction a list of certified interpreters and shall make that list available upon request.  The office of the superintendent of public instruction shall send a list of all certified interpreters who have successfully completed the training to each school district every year.

 

    NEW SECTION.  Sec. 8.  The office of deaf and hard of hearing services, or its successor authority, shall administer this chapter.

 

    NEW SECTION.  Sec. 9.  Violation of or aiding in violation of any part of section 2 of this act constitutes a class 1 civil infraction.

 

    NEW SECTION.  Sec. 10.  This act takes effect December 1, 1999.

 

    NEW SECTION.  Sec. 11.  Sections 1 through 10 of this act constitute a new chapter in Title 72 RCW.
