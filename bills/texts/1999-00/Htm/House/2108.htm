  Preserving moderate-income housing.


    AN ACT Relating to preservation of moderate-income housing; amending RCW 43.84.092 and 43.84.092; adding new sections to chapter 43.180 RCW; creating a new section; making an appropriation; providing an effective date; and providing an expiration date.

 

BE IT ENACTED BY THE LEGISLATURE OF THE STATE OF WASHINGTON:

 

    NEW SECTION.  Sec. 1.  The legislature finds that there is a severe shortage of affordable housing within the state of Washington and that preservation of the existing supply of affordable housing is a cost-effective alternative to displacement of tenants.

    It is the purpose of this act to preserve multifamily rental housing in the state of Washington and to minimize the involuntary displacements of tenants currently residing in such housing.

 

    NEW SECTION.  Sec. 2.  A new section is added to chapter 43.180 RCW to read as follows:

    The commission, in cooperation with the department of community, trade, and economic development, shall develop and implement a rental housing preservation program that:

    (1) Provides subsidized or unsubsidized mortgage financing for multifamily rental housing;

    (2) Provides flexible loan underwriting guidelines, including but not limited to provisions that encourage ownership of multifamily housing by nonprofit organizations;

    (3) Gives priority funding, through the commission, to multifamily rental housing projects that result in the long-term preservation of housing for moderate-income households;

    (4) Targets existing public and private resources to assist in the new construction, rehabilitation, or acquisition of multifamily rental housing for moderate-income households; and

    (5) Uses a portion of the revenue generated from the issuance of bonds under this chapter to assist in the new construction, rehabilitation, or acquisition of multifamily rental housing for moderate-income households.

 

    NEW SECTION.  Sec. 3.  A new section is added to chapter 43.180 RCW to read as follows:

    (1) The rental housing preservation program developed under section 2 of this act shall:

    (a) Be limited to housing units that are owned or will be owned by nonprofit organizations, associations, or corporations, with priority given to nonprofit tenant organizations;

    (b) Give priority to nonprofit organizations, associations, or corporations that will operate the housing as housing for moderate-income households for a period of at least fifty years and will agree to a written covenant agreeing to keep rents affordable to moderate-income households;

    (c) Give priority to multifamily rental housing projects that are located in metropolitan areas that have experienced sharp increases in rents; and

    (d) To the extent feasible, use the funds in the moderate-income housing preservation account created in section 4 of this act to assist nonprofit organizations, associations, or corporations in the preservation of housing for moderate-income households.

    (2) As used in this section and section 2 of this act, "moderate-income households" means a single person, family, or unrelated persons living together whose adjusted income is greater than fifty percent but less than eighty percent of the median family income, adjusted for household size, for the county where the project is located.

 

    NEW SECTION.  Sec. 4.  A new section is added to chapter 43.180 RCW to read as follows:

    The moderate-income housing preservation account is created in the state treasury.  All receipts from appropriations by the legislature, private contributions, repayment of loans made from this account, and all other sources must be deposited into the account.  Moneys in the account may be spent only after appropriation.  Expenditures from the account must be used for the purposes of chapter . . ., Laws of 1999 (this act).

 

    NEW SECTION.  Sec. 5.  A new section is added to chapter 43.180 RCW to read as follows:

    The commission, in consultation with the department of community, trade, and economic development, may provide technical assistance to nonprofit tenant organizations who wish to convert the multifamily housing development in which they reside to tenant ownership.  Technical assistance does not include details connected with the sale or conversion of a multifamily housing development which would require the commission to act in a representative capacity.
