BE IT ENACTED BY THE LEGISLATURE OF THE STATE OF WASHINGTON:

NEW SECTION.  Sec. 1   The legislature finds that the forgotten component of the 1993 education reform legislation was that the legislature was going to provide greater flexibility for school districts and educators. The legislature further finds that the legislature has made several unsuccessful attempts to whittle away at the state mandates upon local school districts. The legislature intends to improve public education in Washington by strengthening local control and repealing the state education code, except to provide each school board of directors broad, discretionary power to enable school districts to be efficient and effective in teaching the public school students of Washington state. The legislature further intends that all future state laws or rules addressing education be grounded in a philosophy of local control.

     (a) The broad discretionary power to determine and adopt written policies not in conflict with other law that provide for the development and implementation of programs, activities, services, or practices that the board determines will:
     (i) Promote the education and daily physical activity of kindergarten through twelfth grade students in the public schools; or
     (ii) Promote the effective, efficient, or safe management and operation of the school district;
     (b) Such powers as are expressly authorized by law; and
     (c) Such powers as are necessarily or fairly implied in the powers expressly authorized by law.
     (2) Before adopting a policy under subsection (1)(a) of this section, the school district board of directors shall comply with the notice requirements of the open public meetings act, chapter 42.30 RCW, and shall in addition include in that notice a statement that sets forth or reasonably describes the proposed policy. The board of directors shall provide a reasonable opportunity for public written and oral comment and consideration of the comment by the board of directors.

NEW SECTION.  Sec. 4   Interested stakeholders are encouraged to inform the legislature as to any additional legislation that needs to be enacted before December 1, 2010, to enable school districts to be efficient and effective in teaching the public school students of Washington state when the other statutes are repealed in 2011.

NEW SECTION.  Sec. 5   Section 3 of this act takes effect May 1, 2011.
