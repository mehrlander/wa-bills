Prohibiting harassment through electronic communication.


    AN ACT Relating to harassment; amending RCW 9.61.230 and 9A.46.020; and prescribing penalties.

 

BE IT ENACTED BY THE LEGISLATURE OF THE STATE OF WASHINGTON:

 

    Sec. 1.  RCW 9.61.230 and 1992 c 186 s 6 are each amended to read as follows:

    Every person who, with intent to harass, intimidate, torment or embarrass any other person, shall make a telephone call or send an electronic communication to such other person:

    (1) Using any lewd, lascivious, ((profane,)) indecent, or obscene words or language, or suggesting the commission of any lewd or lascivious act; or

    (2) Anonymously or repeatedly or at an extremely inconvenient hour, whether or not conversation ensues; or

    (3) Threatening to inflict injury on the person or property of the person ((called)) or any member of his or her family or household;

shall be guilty of a gross misdemeanor, except that the person is guilty of a class C felony if ((either)) any of the following applies:

    (a) That person has previously been convicted of any crime of harassment, as defined in RCW 9A.46.060, with the same victim or member of the victim's family or household or any person specifically named in a no-contact or no-harassment order in this or any other state; ((or))

    (b) ((That)) The person harasses another person under subsection (3) of this section by threatening to kill the person threatened or any other person; or

    (c) The person's victim is or was an employee of a nonprofit organization whose primary purpose is the protection of, or advocacy on behalf of, victims of crime, and the person is harassing the victim in retaliation for an act the victim performed during the course of such duties or to influence the victim's performance of such duties.

 

    Sec. 2.  RCW 9A.46.020 and 1999 c 27 s 2 are each amended to read as follows:

    (1) A person is guilty of harassment if:

    (a) Without lawful authority, the person knowingly threatens:

    (i) To cause bodily injury immediately or in the future to the person threatened or to any other person; or

    (ii) To cause physical damage to the property of a person other than the actor; or

    (iii) To subject the person threatened or any other person to physical confinement or restraint; or

    (iv) Maliciously to do any other act, including sending an electronic communication to the person threatened, or publishing, republishing, reproducing and/or distributing in print or electronic form personally identifiable information about the person, which is intended to substantially harm the person threatened or another with respect to his or her physical or mental health or safety; and

    (b) The person by words or conduct places the person threatened in reasonable fear that the threat will be carried out.  "Words or conduct" includes, in addition to any other form of communication or conduct, taking or appearing to take photographs as defined in RCW 9A.44.115, and the sending of an electronic communication.

    (2) A person who harasses another is guilty of a gross misdemeanor punishable under chapter 9A.20 RCW, except that the person is guilty of a class C felony if ((either)) any of the following applies:

    (a) The person has previously been convicted in this or any other state of any crime of harassment, as defined in RCW 9A.46.060, of the same victim or members of the victim's family or household or any person specifically named in a no-contact or no-harassment order; ((or))

    (b) The person harasses another person under subsection (1)(a)(i) of this section by threatening to kill the person threatened or any other person; or

    (c) The person's victim is or was an employee of a nonprofit organization whose primary purpose is the protection of, or advocacy on behalf of, victims of crime, and the person is harassing the victim in retaliation for an act the victim performed during the course of such duties or to influence the victim's performance of such duties.

    (3) The penalties provided in this section for harassment do not preclude the victim from seeking any other remedy otherwise available under law.
