Enacting the Congestion Reduction Act of 2000.

    AN ACT Relating to transportation congestion reduction; amending RCW 43.17.020, 47.01.021, 47.01.031, 47.01.041, 47.01.071, and 47.01.250; reenacting and amending RCW 47.01.101; adding a new chapter to Title 47 RCW; creating a new section; repealing RCW 47.01.051, 47.01.061, 47.05.010, 47.05.021, 47.05.030, 47.05.035, 47.05.051, and 47.05.090; and providing effective dates.

 

BE IT ENACTED BY THE LEGISLATURE OF THE STATE OF WASHINGTON:

 

    NEW SECTION.  Sec. 1.  The legislature recognizes that the people of the state rank traffic congestion as one of their greatest concerns and rightfully expect the state to seek realistic, implementable solutions.  Recent studies for Puget Sound have indicated that the annual cost of congestion exceeds two billion dollars a year with a national study ranking the region as third worse in the nation.  Congestion symptoms are becoming evident in parts of Eastern Washington and Southwest Washington as well.  As a result, the legislature declares that reducing traffic congestion while increasing mobility is the primary objective for state and local surface transportation programs.

    Nevertheless, the state's priority programming for highway development has arguably been retarded by conflicting goals, with the result that financial resources have been allocated in a manner that has allowed congestion to reach unacceptable levels.  Congestion has raised not only the accident and injury potential but also dramatically impacted freight movement and service vehicle accessibility.  While planners had hoped that concentrated alternatives such as transit would have had a far greater impact, these solutions at best will have less than five percent of total daily trips by 2010 in Puget Sound, even with the Sound Transit proposal.

    The legislature refocuses the state-wide responsibilities under this title to a unified goal of reducing congestion while increasing mobility by twenty-five percent by the year 2010 for areas outlined under this chapter that exceed the Texas Transportation Institute index or such other congestion index approved by the legislature.  The plan will devote resources to reducing the index to a level that occurred in the early 1980's for those noncompliance areas while focusing on preemptive projects for other regions of the state.

 

    NEW SECTION.  Sec. 2.  The definitions in this section apply throughout this chapter.

    (1) "Congestion index" means measures of performance as developed by the Texas Transportation Institute using, but not limited to, delay-based measures of performance (delay per driver), travel rate index (TRI), roadway congestion index (RCI), or such other criteria as approved by the legislature.  The congestion index must be set at the level that occurred in the early 1980's.  If the index uses a measure of performance based on the roadway congestion index (RCI), the index must be set at 0.95 or lower for the highway/principal arterial system.

    (2) "Freeway/expressway" means a state or county road so designated in accordance with criteria established by state and federal agencies.

    (3) "Guidelines" means the criteria developed jointly by the congestion reduction board and the legislature to replace chapter 47.05 RCW and such other statutes in order to accomplish the goals of section 1 of this act.

    (4) "Principal arterial" means a county road or city street so designated in accordance with criteria established by state and federal agencies.

    (5) "Urban monitoring area" means every area in this state designated as an urban monitoring area by the congestion reduction board.

 

    NEW SECTION.  Sec. 3.  (1) The congestion reduction board is created, consisting of twenty-five members appointed by the governor and confirmed by the senate as follows:

    (a) Seven county members, one each from each region comprising the seven regions in section 5(2) of this act;

    (b) Seven city members, one each from each region comprising the seven regions in section 5(2) of this act;

    (c) Five general members as follows:  Two from the Washington state department of transportation, one from the office of financial management, one from the Public Ports Association, and one from a public transit agency; and

    (d) Six members from the private sector.

    (2) The board shall review plans developed by the state, county, and city governments to identify urban monitoring areas and determine compliance with congestion reduction goals of this chapter.  The board may order changes in plans adopted by metropolitan planning organizations (MPO) and regional transportation planning organizations (RTPO) and a reallocation of funds distributed to MPOs and RTPOs by the federal government.  A jurisdiction deemed not to be in compliance will have funds withheld from state and federal sources until the jurisdiction complies with the requirements of this chapter.

 

    NEW SECTION.  Sec. 4.  On June 30, 2001, the transportation commission is abolished and control and administration of the department of transportation are placed under the governor as an executive agency.  The appropriations and expenditure powers of the department are transferred to the congestion reduction board for surface transportation programs, and in respect to surface transportation programs, the department shall function as a planning and preservation and maintenance department.  The surface transportation programs of the transportation improvement board, county road administration board, and freight mobility strategic investment board are consolidated within the congestion reduction board, and those powers are transferred to the congestion reduction board.  Control of dedicated funding sources from the transportation improvement board and the county road administration board are transferred to the congestion reduction board.

 

    NEW SECTION.  Sec. 5.  (1) Before the 2001 legislative session, the congestion reduction board will evaluate the state transportation plan and other plans as adopted by the transportation commission and department of transportation, local plans submitted through the transportation improvement board, the county road administration board, and the freight mobility strategic investment board, and transportation plans developed by metropolitan planning organizations and regional transportation planning organizations.  The congestion reduction board will distinguish between those urban monitoring areas exceeding an established standard of congestion or delay and those not exceeding the standard.  If the standard is based on the Texas Transportation Institute's travel rate index, the standard will be 1.15 (peak period travel times exceeding off-peak times by fifteen percent or more).   If the congestion reduction board uses a delay per driver approach, the standard will be thirty annual hours of delay per driver.  If the congestion reduction board uses the roadway congestion index, the standard will be an index of 1.0.  Those urban monitoring areas exceeding the standard will be designated as "congested," and the congestion reduction board will submit an action plan to the legislature advocating solutions.  For those urban monitoring areas not exceeding the standard, the congestion reduction board will highlight freeway/expressway and principal arterials that might exceed the standard by 2010 unless preemptive action is taken by state or local governments.  For succeeding biennia, the congestion reduction board will monitor and update the plan as necessary.

    (2) For the purpose of analysis, the urban monitoring areas of the state are classified as follows:  (a) Puget Sound including areas within King, Pierce, Snohomish, and Kitsap counties; (b) Olympic region including Clallam, Jefferson, Grays Harbor, Mason, and Thurston counties; (c) Northwest region including Whatcom, Skagit, Island, and San Juan counties; (d) Southwest region including Clark, Cowlitz, Wahkiakum, Pacific, Lewis, Skamania, and Klickitat counties; (e) North central region including Okanogan, Chelan, Douglas, Grant, and Ferry counties; (f) South central region including Kittitas, Yakima, Benton, Franklin, Walla Walla, Columbia, Garfield, and Asotin counties; (g) Eastern region including Stevens, Pend Oreille, Lincoln, Spokane, Adams, and Whitman counties.

 

    NEW SECTION.  Sec. 6.  Funds available for expenditure by the congestion reduction board under this chapter and apportioned to the seven regions of the state must be budgeted and spent, under proper appropriations, for specific highway or road improvement projects within the urban monitoring areas of each region in accordance with the guidelines.  These expenditures in urban monitoring areas are in addition to expenditures from all other construction funds regularly programmed before the effective date of this section for highway improvements throughout the state under chapter 47.05 RCW.

 

    NEW SECTION.  Sec. 7.  Funds available for expenditure by the congestion reduction board must be apportioned to the seven regions for expenditure upon freeways/expressways and local roads in the seven regions in a manner that accomplishes the overall objective of section 1 of this act.  Necessary funds must be spent to achieve congestion reduction under the standard index regardless of the overall distribution of projected expenditures state-wide.

 

    NEW SECTION.  Sec. 8.  Under this chapter, city and county governments shall dedicate required levels of expenditures to achieve congestion reduction on principal arterials, arterials, roads, and local highways under their jurisdiction in order to receive funds from state and federal sources for surface transportation programs including, but not limited to, transit, ferries, rail, bicycle, or any activity using state and local highway and road systems.  In addition, metropolitan planning organizations distributing federal or other funds to city or county governments may not do so unless the congestion reduction board has declared the city or county government is in compliance with this chapter.
