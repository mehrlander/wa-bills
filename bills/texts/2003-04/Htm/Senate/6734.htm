     AN ACT Relating to providing certainty and clarity in the administration of water rights; amending RCW 90.03.380; reenacting and amending RCW 90.14.140; adding new sections to chapter 90.03 RCW; adding a new section to chapter 90.44 RCW; adding a new chapter to Title 90 RCW; creating a new section; and repealing RCW 90.14.130, 90.14.160, 90.14.170, 90.14.180, 90.14.190, 90.14.200, 90.14.210, 90.14.215, and 90.14.230.

BE IT ENACTED BY THE LEGISLATURE OF THE STATE OF WASHINGTON:

NEW SECTION.  Sec. 1   (1) The legislature finds that the present water rights management system is not able to function quickly and efficiently. This is in part due to the variety of types of water rights, including many water rights based on claims established prior to the creation of a statutory permit process, and uncertainty raised by court decisions affecting administration of the water code in light of this variety of rights.
     Therefore, the legislature declares that the creation of a water rights confirmation process is critical to the economic viability of the state. The goal of this confirmation process is to provide certainty and clarity to water right holders so that the administration of the water code is more effective and expedient while ensuring that water right holders are ensured due process. This process will result in faster, simpler water right transactions, provide a clarified picture of actual water use, and provide greater certainty as to the effect of water withdrawals and diversions on stream flow levels.
     (2) The legislature finds that the present system for determining that a water right holder has relinquished or abandoned a water right creates disincentives for using water efficiently, and greatly contributes to the uncertainty in the use of water rights and to the administration of an effective water management system.
     Therefore, the legislature declares that this system should be reformed by repealing the current law of relinquishment and by reforming the law of abandonment to create a system that continues to require continuous beneficial use of water rights, but also provides certainty and flexibility of use for water right holders and state water managers.
     (3)(a) The legislature finds that the present provisions of the water code are ambiguous regarding whether, in accordance with the relinquishment provisions, the mere nonuse of an otherwise valid water right, without any other action, results in the relinquishment of that water right and creates a corresponding right of use in other users. The legislature finds that this ambiguity creates unacceptable levels of uncertainty for water right holders and state water managers.
     (b) The legislature further finds that water right holders are entitled to due process under the Washington state Constitution prior to any determination that a water right has relinquished.
     (c) Therefore, the legislature declares that to ensure an effective and efficient transition to a new water rights confirmation process, water rights are not considered relinquished unless determined to be so by a court of competent jurisdiction or an administrative process subject to judicial review. Any water rights determined to be relinquished by such a court revert to the state for appropriation in accordance with the water code.
     (4)(a) The legislature further finds that clear and consistent standards for changes and transfers are essential to ensure that existing water rights are an effective tool for water management.
     (b) Therefore, the legislature declares that the use of water to irrigate additional acres or supply additional uses may take place as long as such a change in the water right does not cause detriment or injury to any other water right. Additionally, the legislature declares that alternatives to the consumptive use test for water spreading to additional lands are essential to achieve flexibility.
     (5)(a) Further, the legislature finds that certain water uses necessitate greater flexibility and that such uses are currently exempt from relinquishment. These uses include: Power development, standby or reserve water supplies, municipal water supply, federal irrigation projects, water held in trust, and permits still under development.
     (b) Therefore, the legislature declares that these water uses, and ground water withdrawals exempt from permitting requirements, are not subject to the water rights confirmation process established in this chapter, unless the holder of the water right elects to subject that right to the confirmation process.

NEW SECTION.  Sec. 2   (1) Within one hundred eighty days of the effective date of this section, the presiding judge of the water court, or until establishment of a water court, the chief judges of the Washington court of appeals, shall appoint three or more referees as needed to process water rights under the water rights confirmation process established by this chapter.
     (2) Each referee shall consult with the department of ecology to establish a water quantity confirmation schedule for each water resource inventory area of the state. The water rights confirmation process created in this chapter must be completed by December 31, 2014, for each water resource inventory area.
     (3) As of the effective date of this section, all state-based water rights are subject to the water rights confirmation process, except those rights described in: RCW 90.14.140 (13), (14), (15), (16), (17), and (20); rights exempt from permit requirements under RCW 90.44.050; or permits still under development as described in RCW 90.14.150.
     (4) Any holder of a right described in RCW 90.14.140 (13), (14), (15), (16), and (17) may choose to have such right confirmed under the provisions of this chapter. Only the portion of the water right actually put to beneficial use may be confirmed. Any portion of the right not put to beneficial use may not be confirmed or altered by the confirmation process established in this chapter.
     (5) Water rights exempt from permit requirements under RCW 90.44.050 or permits still under development as described in RCW 90.14.150 may be confirmed as to quantity under the provisions of this act. Only the portion of the water right put to beneficial use may be confirmed. Any portion of the right not put to beneficial use may not be confirmed or altered by the confirmation process established in this chapter.

NEW SECTION.  Sec. 3   Within ninety days of the effective date of this section, the department of ecology shall issue notice to all water right holders of record through direct mailing and through general means such as newspaper listings and internet postings. The notice shall include: (1) A brief description of the water rights confirmation process created by this chapter; and (2) a request that each water right holder file current contact information with the department of ecology by July 1, 2005.
     The contact information must include the current name of the water right holder, mailing address, telephone numbers, electronic mail address, if any, and the tax parcel number of the land to which the water right is appurtenant.
     The department of ecology shall make the contact information available to the referees appointed under section 2 of this act.

NEW SECTION.  Sec. 4   (1) Each referee shall issue notice of opportunity to confirm water right quantities to all water right holders within their assigned jurisdiction. The notice must be made through direct mailing to water right holders of record, and through general means such as newspaper listings and internet postings. The notice must also include information related to any technical assistance available to water right holders to aid in complying with the provisions of this section.
     (2) Upon receipt of such a notice, but no later than the time specified in the notice, all water right holders subject to the water rights confirmation process shall file a statement of water use with the referee. The statement of use must include: (a) The highest instantaneous quantity diverted or withdrawn during the period from January 1, 1994, through December 31, 2003; (b) the highest annual quantity diverted or withdrawn during the period from January 1, 1994, through December 31, 2003; (c) documentation showing that the quantities in (a) and (b) of this subsection were put to beneficial use; (d) the basis for withdrawal or diversion of water, including the permit number, certificate number, claim filing number, or other statutory authority for the right; and (e) the quantities authorized by the permit, certificate, claim, or other statutory authority.
     (3) Documentation under subsection (2)(c) of this section may include power meter records, water meter records, crop and acreage records, or any other information to show the quantity of water used. For rights that embody quantities of water use under one-tenth of a cubic foot per second, the referee shall develop simplified procedures such as a checklist for submitting evidence of use. For purposes of this section, the highest instantaneous and annual quantities may occur in different years.
     (4)(a) If the water right holder did not fully exercise a water right from January 1, 1994, through December 31, 2003, the water right holder may document the reasons for the nonuse in accordance with subsections (2) and (3) of this section. If the reason for the nonuse was valid based on RCW 90.14.140, the water right holder may document the highest instantaneous quantity and annual quantity put to beneficial use during the ten-year period immediately preceding the period of nonuse.
     (b) For rights described in RCW 90.14.140 (13), (14), (15), (16), and (17), the instantaneous quantity and annual quantity shall be the highest quantities put to beneficial use.
     (5) Those water rights required to be confirmed under section 2(3) of this act, for which a statement of water use is not filed within the time frame established in subsection (1) of this section, are presumed by the referee to be abandoned. The right must be reported to the water court if one has been established or the superior court of competent jurisdiction. The court shall annually hear such reports and find that the rights have been abandoned, unless the water right holder can prove otherwise.

NEW SECTION.  Sec. 5   (1) The referee shall examine the documentation submitted for a right under section 4 of this act. The examination may only consider if the:
     (a) Right is based on a permit, certificate, or claim filed in accordance with chapter 90.14 RCW, or other statutory authority;
     (b) Beneficial use is consistent with the place of use, purpose of use, point of diversion or withdrawal, or season of use contained in the permit, certificate, or claim filed in accordance with chapter 90.14 RCW, or other statutory authority;
     (c) Instantaneous quantity and annual quantity put to beneficial use are within the quantity authorized by the permit, certificate, claim, or other statutory authority;
     (d) Reason for the nonuse, if any, was valid based on RCW 90.14.140, for water rights addressed in section 4(3) of this act; or
     (e) Water use in the filing is accurate based on the documentation provided.
     (2)(a) By exception and for good cause, the referee may conduct field investigations of a filing to ensure that the requirements of subsection (1) of this section are met. The referee may not hold evidentiary hearings. A filing may be further investigated and evidentiary hearings may be held only if the referee's decision is appealed in accordance with subsection (4) of this section, at the direction of the court.
     (b) If any filing is incomplete or appears to be inaccurate, the referee shall return it to the water right holder with an explanation of what additional information is needed.
     (c) If a water right embodied in a filing requires changes to properly reflect the current place of use, purpose of use, point of diversion or withdrawal, or season of use, the referee shall direct the water right holder to apply to the department of ecology or a water conservancy board for such change in accordance with section 11 of this act.
     (3) The referee shall issue a record of decision consistent with sections 2 through 4 of this act and twice each month the referee shall publish a notice reflecting the record of decision of rights confirmed since issuance of the last notice and provide copies of such a notice to water right holders in the same water resource inventory area.
     (4)(a) Any aggrieved water right holder in the same water resource inventory area or relying on the same source of supply, including the holder of the right for which a record of decision is issued in accordance with subsection (3) of this section, may file an appeal of the record of decision within thirty days of publication under the provisions of subsection (3) of this section.
     (b) The appeal must be filed with the water court if established, or until such time as it is established, with the superior court of competent jurisdiction.
     (c) For purposes of this section, the department of ecology is deemed a water right holder for all instream flows established by rule and trust water rights acquired under the authority of chapter 90.38 or 90.42 RCW. This subsection (4)(c) does not affect the authority of the department to regulate existing water rights through the exercise of regulatory powers granted to the department.
     (5) If no appeal is filed, the referee shall issue a superseding document reflecting the confirmed quantities. The superseding document does not change the type of underlying right. A superseding claim is issued for a water right claim, a superseding permit is issued for a water right permit, and a superseding certificate is issued for a water right certificate.
     (6)(a) If an appeal is filed, the court may only examine whether the record of decision is in accordance with the provisions of subsection (1) of this section.
     (b) The burden of proof is on the appellant to show that the record of decision is inaccurate.
     (c) The court may conduct evidentiary hearings or field investigations to supplement the record of decision as needed to determine if the record of decision is accurate.
     (d) The decision of the court will serve as the new confirmed quantity for the water right, and the court shall direct the referee to issue the appropriate superceding document in accordance with subsection (5) of this section.

NEW SECTION.  Sec. 6   (1)(a) Except as provided in (d) of this subsection, once a right has been confirmed in accordance with section 4 of this act, the holder of the right must annually submit a notice of water use to the department of ecology showing measured or estimated beneficial use from the previous year and planned beneficial use of the right during the current calendar year. The notice must be filed by May 1st of each year. The department of ecology shall mail a reminder to each holder of a confirmed water right that the annual notice is due no later than May 1st, and shall provide the holder with a notice card for reporting annual use under the right.
     (b) The annual notice must include how the water will be used, and if any amount of the confirmed right is not planned to be put to use, the water right holder must designate whether such water will be transferred to another user or donated, leased, or sold to the state trust water right program.
     (c) Water right holders may file by mail, electronic mail, or through the internet. The department of ecology shall keep the notices and post them on an internet site categorized by watershed.
     (d) For the purposes of this section, annual notice of water use for municipal water suppliers means the reports provided to the department of health as required by chapter 70.119A RCW.
     (2)(a) The department of ecology shall monitor and review submitted notices of water use to ensure the notices are complete and may require the water right holder to correct incomplete notices.
     (b) If a water right holder fails to file an annual notice of water use for a confirmed water right for five consecutive years, the department of ecology must notify the water right holder stating that failure to file such a notice of water use for ten consecutive years constitutes abandonment of the right. The department of ecology shall annually provide such a notice until a notice of water use is filed in accordance with subsection (1) of this section.
     (3) If a notice of water use for a right confirmed under the provisions of section 4 of this act is not filed for ten consecutive years, the right is deemed abandoned on December 31st of the tenth year. Such a right must be reported to the water court if one has been established or the superior court of competent jurisdiction. The court shall annually hear such reports and find that the rights have been abandoned, unless the water right holder can prove that the notices were filed.

NEW SECTION.  Sec. 7   The department of ecology may issue grants to public bodies or private associations to provide technical assistance to water right holders in preparing the initial statement of water use or the annual notice of water use, or both. The grants may be made from funds appropriated by the legislature for that purpose.

Sec. 8   RCW 90.03.380 and 2003 c 329 s 2 are each amended to read as follows:
     (1) The right to the use of water which has been applied to a beneficial use in the state shall be and remain appurtenant to the land or place upon which the same is used: PROVIDED, HOWEVER, That the right may be transferred to another or to others and become appurtenant to any other land or place of use without loss of priority of right theretofore established if such change can be made without detriment or injury to existing rights. The point of diversion of water for beneficial use or the purpose of use may be changed, if such change can be made without detriment or injury to existing rights. ((A change in the place of use, point of diversion, and/or purpose of use of a water right to enable irrigation of additional acreage or the addition of new uses may be permitted if such change results in no increase in the annual consumptive quantity of water used under the water right. For purposes of this section, "annual consumptive quantity" means the estimated or actual annual amount of water diverted pursuant to the water right, reduced by the estimated annual amount of return flows, averaged over the two years of greatest use within the most recent five-year period of continuous beneficial use of the water right.)) Before any transfer of such right to use water or change of the point of diversion of water or change of purpose of use can be made, any person having an interest in the transfer or change, shall file a written application therefor with the department, and the application shall not be granted until notice of the application is published as provided in RCW 90.03.280. If it shall appear that such transfer or such change may be made without injury or detriment to existing rights, the department shall issue to the applicant a certificate in duplicate granting the right for such transfer or for such change of point of diversion or of use. The certificate so issued shall be filed and be made a record with the department and the duplicate certificate issued to the applicant may be filed with the county auditor in like manner and with the same effect as provided in the original certificate or permit to divert water.
     (2) If an application for change proposes to transfer water rights from one irrigation district to another, the department shall, before publication of notice, receive concurrence from each of the irrigation districts that such transfer or change will not adversely affect the ability to deliver water to other landowners or impair the financial integrity of either of the districts.
     (3) A change in place of use by an individual water user or users of water provided by an irrigation district need only receive approval for the change from the board of directors of the district if the use of water continues within the irrigation district, and when water is provided by an irrigation entity that is a member of a board of joint control created under chapter 87.80 RCW, approval need only be received from the board of joint control if the use of water continues within the area of jurisdiction of the joint board and the change can be made without detriment or injury to existing rights.
     (4) This section shall not apply to trust water rights acquired by the state through the funding of water conservation projects under chapter 90.38 RCW or RCW 90.42.010 through 90.42.070.
     (5)(a) Pending applications for new water rights are not entitled to protection from impairment, injury, or detriment when an application relating to an existing surface or ground water right is considered.
     (b) Applications relating to existing surface or ground water rights may be processed and decisions on them rendered independently of processing and rendering decisions on pending applications for new water rights within the same source of supply without regard to the date of filing of the pending applications for new water rights.
     (c) Notwithstanding any other existing authority to process applications, including but not limited to the authority to process applications under WAC 173-152-050 as it existed on January 1, 2001, an application relating to an existing surface or ground water right may be processed ahead of a previously filed application relating to an existing right when sufficient information for a decision on the previously filed application is not available and the applicant for the previously filed application is sent written notice that explains what information is not available and informs the applicant that processing of the next application will begin. The previously filed application does not lose its priority date and if the information is provided by the applicant within sixty days, the previously filed application shall be processed at that time. This subsection (5)(c) does not affect any other existing authority to process applications.
     (d) Nothing in this subsection (5) is intended to stop the processing of applications for new water rights.
     (6) No applicant for a change, transfer, or amendment of a water right may be required to give up any part of the applicant's valid water right or claim to a state agency, the trust water rights program, or to other persons as a condition of processing the application.
     (7) In revising the provisions of this section and adding provisions to this section by chapter 237, Laws of 2001, the legislature does not intend to imply legislative approval or disapproval of any existing administrative policy regarding, or any existing administrative or judicial interpretation of, the provisions of this section not expressly added or revised.
     (8) The development and use of a small irrigation impoundment, as defined in RCW 90.03.370(8), does not constitute a change or amendment for the purposes of this section. The exemption expressly provided by this subsection shall not be construed as requiring a change or transfer of any existing water right to enable the holder of the right to store water governed by the right.

NEW SECTION.  Sec. 9   A new section is added to chapter 90.03 RCW to read as follows:
     A holder of a confirmed water right may transfer any portion of the right to another person, to additional lands, or to a different use if the change does not impair another water right under the procedures in RCW 90.03.380. In reviewing proposed changes that would enable irrigation of additional acres beyond the number of acres authorized by the right or the addition of new uses to a water right, the department shall evaluate and determine whether the proposed change would increase the average annual consumptive quantity of water use in comparison to the authorized use as confirmed under the right. If the proposed change would increase the average annual consumptive quantity of water used under the right, the department must determine and ensure that other existing water rights are not impaired as a result of the water right change.

NEW SECTION.  Sec. 10   A new section is added to chapter 90.44 RCW to read as follows:
     A holder of a confirmed water right may transfer any portion of the right to another person, to additional lands, or to a different use if the change does not impair another water right under the procedures in RCW 90.44.100. In reviewing proposed changes that would enable irrigation of additional acres beyond the number of acres authorized by the right or the addition of new uses to a water right, the department shall evaluate and determine whether the proposed change would increase the average annual consumptive quantity of water use in comparison to the authorized use as confirmed under the right. If the proposed change would increase the average annual consumptive quantity of water used under the right, the department must determine and ensure that other existing water rights are not impaired as a result of the water right change.

NEW SECTION.  Sec. 11   (1) A surface or ground water right must be confirmed by the process established in this chapter prior to change or transfer of a water right after the effective date of this section. If a water right proposed for change or transfer has not been confirmed, the water right holder may choose to have the right confirmed in accordance with section 2 of this act or by the department of ecology or a conservancy board, except when the referee has directed the right to the department of ecology or conservancy board under the provisions of section 5(2)(c) of this act.
     (2) If the water right holder chooses to use the department of ecology or a conservancy board, the department of ecology or conservancy board shall apply the confirmation standards as provided in sections 4 and 5 of this act, and shall provide a written report to the referee detailing the water use as reported by the water right holder. The referee need not be bound by any findings in the report, and shall complete the confirmation process in accordance with the provisions of section 5 of this act.
     (3) The court, the department of ecology, and water conservancy boards must develop procedures to ensure that changes and transfers to water rights are not delayed during the confirmation process.
     (4) When evaluating water rights for the purpose of change or transfer of the right, the department of ecology shall rely on the quantity as confirmed by the referee.
     (5) Prior to the appointment of a referee for a watershed, water right changes and transfers issued by the department of ecology or local water conservancy boards and new certificates for permitted water rights must be annotated to indicate that confirmation of the quantity of the right will be conducted upon appointment of a referee. Upon appointment of a referee, the department of ecology will submit copies of all water right change decisions and certificates for new water rights issued since the effective date of this section to the appropriate referee for independent review and confirmation of the quantity of the right.

NEW SECTION.  Sec. 12   The provisions of this chapter do not apply to surface water rights and claims already undergoing adjudication in the Yakima basin for which final orders or conditional final orders have not yet been issued. However, surface water rights and claims for which final orders or conditional final orders have been issued are subject to the provisions of sections 6, 7, 9, 10, and 11(4) of this act and RCW 90.03.380 and 90.14.140, and with respect to quantity are equivalent to rights confirmed under the provisions of this chapter.

NEW SECTION.  Sec. 14   A new section is added to chapter 90.03 RCW to read as follows:
     For water rights that have previously been confirmed as to quantity under sections 4 and 5 of this act, and have not been abandoned under section 6 of this act, a court conducting a water rights adjudication shall adopt the previously confirmed quantity. For water rights that have not been confirmed as to quantity under sections 4 and 5 of this act, the court shall review and establish the valid quantity of the right based on the standards and requirements of sections 4 and 5 of this act. For rights that cannot be quantified based on beneficial use, the court shall apply legally applicable standards.

NEW SECTION.  Sec. 15   Section 13 of this act applies to all causes of action commenced on or after the effective date of this section, regardless of when the cause of action arose. To this extent, section 13 of this act applies retroactively, but in all other respects it applies prospectively.
